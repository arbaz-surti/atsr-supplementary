## What participation involves

You are invited to contribute professional QA judgments to an evaluation of
AI-generated test-scope recommendations. Participation has two parts:

1. independently assess 18 anonymized software specifications to identify an
   appropriate coverage depth and essential test-scope elements; and
2. after a consensus reference and scoring rubric are finalized, independently rate
   a blinded sample of 162 generated recommendations on five 1–5 dimensions.

The expected total time is approximately 8–15 hours, depending on review depth. Work
may be split across sessions. The researcher will not ask you to disclose confidential
employer, customer, or project information.

## Data collected and use

The study will collect your judgments, rubric scores, optional scoring notes, and a
limited professional-background description used to establish rater qualifications.
The manuscript, supplementary materials, and research archive may report or release
these data under an anonymized participant code. Your name, signature, email address,
employer, and other direct identifiers will not be included in the manuscript or
public research data.

The results may be used in the revised Scientific Reports manuscript, peer-review
responses, supplementary files, and a public reproducibility archive. The journal
may request documentation showing that informed consent was obtained. Identifying
consent records will be retained privately and will not be placed in the public
archive.

## Voluntary participation and withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. You may decline any question or stop participating
without penalty. You may request withdrawal of your identifiable and coded responses
before the researcher confirms in writing that the rating dataset has been locked.
After de-identification, aggregation, or public release, it may no longer be possible
to identify and remove individual records from every archived copy.

The activity is expected to pose minimal risk beyond ordinary professional review
work. A possible risk is unintended identification through records or unusually
specific notes. The researcher will reduce this risk by using participant codes,
removing direct identifiers from research data, and keeping consent records separate.
There is no guaranteed personal benefit. The study may improve evidence about how
AI-generated QA recommendations should be evaluated.
